BIFF co-parenting messages

A simple structure for high-conflict texts: brief, informative, friendly, and firm—without fueling the fight.

Home / BIFF co-parenting messages

BIFF means Brief, Informative, Friendly, Firm—a practical pattern for high-conflict co-parenting messages. Use it when you need a paper trail without a fight.

Not legal advice. Match your orders and counsel. This is communication help only.

What BIFF stands for

  • Brief — one topic, short paragraphs, no essays.
  • Informative — facts, times, places, what you need.
  • Friendly — civil tone (not fake warmth). No sarcasm.
  • Firm — clear boundary or ask; don’t invite debate.

Before → after examples

Schedule change

Before

“You always dump changes on me last minute. I’m done being flexible when you never are.”

BIFF

“For Friday’s pickup, I can do 5:00 or 5:45 at school. Please confirm one by Thursday noon so we can plan for [Child].”

Blame about money

Before

“You refuse to pay anything and act like the kids don’t cost money. Typical.”

BIFF

“Soccer registration is $180 due [date]. I’ve attached the invoice. Please send your half ($90) by [date] via [method], or propose another split in writing.”

How to write a BIFF message in 60 seconds

  1. Delete insults, mind-reading, and history dumps.
  2. Keep only logistics + one ask + deadline if needed.
  3. Read it once as if a judge or the kids might see it later.
  4. If it still feels hot, run Tone Guardian or a rewrite in Gentler Coparent.

BIFF vs Message Shield

  • BIFF — a structure you apply when writing.
  • Message Shield — help when they sent something hard: heat check + calm reply.
  • Tone Guardian — cool your draft before send.

FAQ

Is BIFF only for high-conflict co-parenting?

It helps most when conflict is high, but brief, factual messages work in low-conflict homes too.

Should every message be BIFF?

Use it when stakes or conflict are high. Logistics still deserve clarity even when things are fine.

Is this legal advice?

No—communication patterns only. Ask a qualified attorney about orders and enforcement.
