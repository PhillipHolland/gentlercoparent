Before you send a co-parenting text

A 60-second pause that protects your kids—and your future self.

Home / Before you send a co-parenting text

The most expensive co-parenting messages are the ones you send in the first 90 seconds of anger. A short pause—and a better draft—can save the rest of the week.

Not legal advice. Communication habits only. Match your orders and professional guidance.

60-second checklist before you hit send

  1. Child first? Does the first sentence help the kids or score points?
  2. One topic? Delete the pile-on.
  3. One ask? What do you need, by when?
  4. Readable later? Would you be okay if a judge, mediator, or your kid read this in five years?
  5. Still hot? Wait, walk, or run Tone Guardian.

Red flags in your draft

  • “You always / you never”
  • Name-calling, sarcasm, all-caps rants
  • Threats you don’t intend to follow through on
  • Kids as messengers or leverage
  • Re-litigating the divorce in a logistics text

Pause and rewrite

Hot draft

“I’m sick of your games. If you can’t get them here on time I’m calling my lawyer.”

After pause

“Please confirm ETA for the 5:30 handoff at school. If you’ll be more than 15 minutes late, text so I can plan for [Child].”

Tools when willpower isn’t enough

  • BIFF — brief, informative, friendly, firm structure
  • Templates — starting points when the box is blank

FAQ

How long should I wait?

Long enough to remove insults and pick one ask. For some people that’s 10 minutes; for others, overnight.

What if it’s truly urgent?

State the emergency fact and the needed action. Save the argument for later—or never.
