Co-parenting expense messages

Ask for money without the lecture—clear amounts, due dates, and one ask.

Home / Co-parenting expense messages

Money texts turn personal fast. Use invoice + amount + due date + method + one ask—not a history of every unfair thing since the divorce.

Not legal advice. Follow your decree, support order, and counsel. Keep records outside the heat of the chat.

What to include

  • What the expense is (activity, medical, school)
  • Total and each parent’s share if agreed
  • Due date and payment method
  • Attachment or link to the bill when you can
  • One clear ask—no side arguments

Copy-ready expense templates

Shared activity fee

[Child]’s [activity] fee is $[total], due [date]. Per our arrangement, your share is $[amount]. Invoice attached. Please send via [Venmo/Zelle/check] by [date] and reply “paid” when sent.

Medical / copay

[Child] had [appointment type] on [date]. Copay was $[amount]. Receipt attached. Please reimburse $[your share] by [date] via [method].

Unreimbursed balance (follow-up)

Following up on the [expense] from [date] ($[amount] your share). Please confirm payment by [date] or propose a written payment date this week so we can close this out for [Child].

Disagreement on whether it’s shared

I believe [expense] is shared under [plan / past practice]. Total $[amount]. If you disagree, please reply with the section of the order you rely on, or a different split proposal, by [date] so we can resolve this without delaying [Child]’s [activity/care].

Heated money text → calmer send

Before

“You never pay for anything and I’m done covering your half while you play hero with the kids.”

After

“Camp deposit is $200 due Friday (invoice attached). Your half is $100. Please send by Friday 5 pm via Zelle so [Child] keeps the spot.”

When the draft is still angry

  1. Strip blame; keep numbers and dates.
  2. One expense per message when possible.
  3. Run Tone Guardian if you’re about to send heat.
  4. If they attack instead of paying, answer only logistics—or document and pause.

If the fight is really about what the order says, ask in-app with decree-aware answers (verify against the signed order).

FAQ

Should I negotiate percentages in text?

State your understanding and ask for a written alternative. Long negotiations by text often escalate—keep options few and dated.

What if they ignore the request?

One calm follow-up with the same numbers and deadline. Then use your agreed process (portal, counsel, court tools)—not a stack of angry messages.

Is this legal advice?

No. Communication samples only.
