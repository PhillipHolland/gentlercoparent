Texas co-parenting messages

Practical, child-first texts for Texas parents—plus an iPhone coach when the draft is hot.

Home / Guides / Texas co-parenting messages

If you co-parent in Texas, logistics often run through possession schedules, school calendars, and a written order. The communication job is the same everywhere: brief, factual, child-first—especially when conflict is high.

Not legal advice. Texas family law is fact-specific. This page is about how to write messages, not how a court will rule. Verify every schedule and support question against your signed order and a Texas family-law attorney.

What Texas co-parents text about most

  • Possession exchanges (weekday, weekend, holiday)
  • School pickup, early release, activities
  • Medical notice and decision logistics
  • Expenses and reimbursements
  • Travel and holiday handoffs

Message rules that travel well in Texas (and anywhere)

  1. Lead with the kids and the logistics—not the marriage.
  2. One topic per message.
  3. Cite the written plan when you disagree (“per the order / calendar…”), without legal lecturing.
  4. Give a deadline when you need a choice (A or B by Thursday noon).
  5. Stop when logistics are clear—even if they keep arguing.

Possession dispute tone

Before

“You always steal my weekends. Texas law is on my side and I’m done.”

After

“Per our written schedule, I have [Child] this weekend starting Friday at 6 pm at [location]. Please confirm the handoff. If you read the order differently, reply with the section you rely on by Thursday noon.”

Templates for common Texas logistics

Weekend possession confirm

Confirming weekend possession for [Child]: Friday [time] pickup at [location] through Sunday [time] return at [location], per our schedule. Text only if something changes.

Holiday handoff

For [holiday], I understand possession is [start] to [end] at [location]. Please confirm by [date]. If your reading differs, send the order section you use so we can align before [Child] is in the middle.

School day exchange

School day handoff: I will pick up [Child] at [school/door] at [time]. If the school calendar changes, let’s update each other the same day in writing.
