Templates

Co-parenting text message templates

Copy-ready examples that stay brief, firm, and child-first—so the next text doesn’t become another fight.

Home / Text message templates

When you’re exhausted, a blank message box is dangerous. These co-parenting text templates give you a calm starting point. Swap in names, times, and places. If the draft still feels hot, run it through Gentler Coparent before you send.

Not legal advice. Match any court order, parenting plan, or professional guidance you have. Adapt for your situation.

The 4-part structure

  1. Child + logistics. Who, what, when—about the kids.
  2. One ask. One topic per message.
  3. Deadline if needed. “By Thursday noon” beats “ASAP.”
  4. Calm close. No sarcasm, no scorekeeping.

Pickup & drop-off

Confirming a scheduled pickup

Confirming I’ll pick up [Child] today at [time] at [location], per the schedule. Please text only if something changes before then.

Running late (your side)

I’m running about [X] minutes behind for pickup. I’ll be at [location] by [new time]. Sorry for the delay—[Child] will still get [snack/homework plan] as planned.

Other parent is late

We’re at [location] for the [time] handoff. Please send an ETA so I can plan for [Child]. If you need a different time, propose two options for next time in writing.

Schedule changes

Requesting a one-time swap

Can we swap [date A] for [date B] for [Child]? If that doesn’t work, please send two alternatives by [day/time] so we can plan.

Declining a last-minute change

I can’t change [date] at this short notice. We’ll stick to the written schedule. For future changes, please ask at least [X days] ahead when possible.

School & activities

Sharing school info

School note for [Child]: [event/meeting] is on [date] at [time] at [place]. I’ll handle [drop-off/pickup] unless you prefer to. Reply only if that doesn’t work.

Homework / supplies

[Child] needs [item/form] by [day]. I’ll take care of it this week unless you’d rather handle it—just confirm by [time].

Money & expenses

Shared expense (neutral)

Receipt for [Child’s expense]: $[amount] on [date] for [item]. Your half is $[half] if we split 50/50 per our arrangement. I can take [method] or follow whatever we already use.

Following up once

Following up on the [date] expense for [Child] ($[half]). Please confirm when you’ve sent it or if you need the receipt again.

Boundaries & tone

Staying on topic

I want to keep this about [Child’s schedule]. Please reply only about [specific logistics]. I’m not available for a longer argument by text.

Ending a spiraling thread

I’ve shared the plan for [Child]. I’m stepping away from this thread. If something about the schedule changes, send the new time/place in one message.

Heated draft → calmer send

Before

“You’re always late and you don’t care about anyone but yourself. This is why the kids are stressed.”

After

“We’re waiting at school for the 5:30 handoff. Please send an ETA. For next week, let’s stick to the written time so [Child] isn’t waiting.”

FAQ

What should I put in a co-parenting text?

Child + logistics, one clear ask, optional deadline, calm close. Skip sarcasm and old fights.

Are these okay for high-conflict situations?

They’re written for tension. Customize to your plan and orders. Not legal advice.

Templates vs Gentler Coparent?

Templates are static. The app rewrites your live draft—or runs Tone Guardian / Message Shield—so the tone fits this message.
